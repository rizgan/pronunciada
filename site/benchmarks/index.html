Benchmarks on real learner speech

Pronunciada, OpenPronounce, Azure Pronunciation Assessment, Lingolix, TonePerfect and Chivox scored the same recordings of language learners in English, Mandarin, Spanish and German. No synthetic speech is used on this page.

The comparison covers complete pronunciation-assessment systems: a library, app or API that takes a recording and the text that was read, and returns scores for sounds, words and the sentence. OpenPronounce is the other open-source system of that kind. Azure, Lingolix, TonePerfect and Chivox are commercial cloud services. Every system received the same audio and text.

Combined score

One number per system: how closely its scores follow human judgement, averaged over the four languages with learner recordings. In English and Mandarin the judges are experts who graded the pronunciation accuracy of each recording. In Spanish and German they are native speakers, whose recordings a system should score above the learners' recordings of the same text. The number is the share of pairs of recordings that a system puts in the same order as the judges: 0.5 is chance and 1 is perfect agreement. Each system is compared with Pronunciada on the languages both of them cover.

Pronunciada has the higher combined score against every system, and the difference is significant against Azure, TonePerfect, Chivox, OpenPronounce distilled and ZIPA alone. Against Lingolix and OpenPronounce the difference is not significant. Over all four languages Pronunciada's combined score is 0.752.

Checks of the construction: weighting the languages by their number of speakers, or scoring every level the judges marked (sounds, words, sentences, marked sounds, Mandarin characters), keeps Pronunciada ahead of every commercial system; against OpenPronounce the difference is not significant in any version (all versions). Pronunciada used the training parts of speechocean762 and OMPAL for its own fitting; the Spanish and German tests are small.

Accuracy and size

The chart places each system by the size of its model files and by its score on four English tests. Cloud services have no download and are drawn at the right.

Pronunciada loads 107 MB of files with its int8 model and 297 MB with the full model. OpenPronounce loads 396 MB with its distilled English model and 2,603 MB in default mode.

On the speechocean762 test set, Pronunciada agrees with the experts more closely than OpenPronounce, OpenPronounce distilled, Azure, Lingolix and ZIPA alone on sounds and words, and each of those differences is significant. OpenPronounce is significantly better at finding the sounds annotators marked as mispronounced: 0.743 and 0.753 against Pronunciada's 0.712 on L2-ARCTIC, and 0.741 and 0.739 against 0.721 on EpaDB.

What these tests do not show

  • The kind of label differs by language. Expert or teacher scores exist for English and Mandarin, and marked sounds for English only. Spanish and German have only the distinction between learners and native speakers, which rewards anything that differs between the two groups, speaking rate included.
  • Several sets have few speakers: 6 in L2-ARCTIC, 20 in EpaDB, 18 learners in OMPAL, 10 learners and 8 native speakers in Heroico, 7 native speakers in LeaP German. One speaker can move such a figure by several points.
  • There is no learner data here for Japanese, Korean, Russian, Portuguese, French or Italian. These tests say nothing about those languages.
  • The commercial systems are incomplete. Azure was not run on German, Lingolix on speechocean762 and Heroico only, TonePerfect on OMPAL and part of speechocean762, and Chivox on a sample of two sets. No other commercial service was run.
  • Pronunciada and OpenPronounce both used speechocean762 to fit their scores. Neither used L2-ARCTIC, EpaDB, Heroico, the Speech Accent Archive or LeaP. TonePerfect states that it used other OMPAL recordings during its development.
  • Disk, memory and time were measured on one laptop. Nothing was measured on a phone.

Method

Versions: Pronunciada 1.1.0, with the full model and the int8 model. OpenPronounce 1.1.0, the latest release on the day of the runs, in default mode and with its distilled English phone model; the full tables also have OpenPronounce 0.4.0, the release that was current when Pronunciada 1.0 came out. Azure Pronunciation Assessment through its REST API, region westus, November 2026. Lingolix API, model 2026-11. TonePerfect API version 2 with fallback off, 24 and 25 November 2026. Chivox speech evaluation API, sentence engines for English and Mandarin. ZIPA alone is zipa-small-crctc-500k with the standard goodness-of-pronunciation score.

Every interval and every difference comes from 10,000 resamples of speakers, with the systems compared on the same resamples. A difference is called significant at p < 0.05, without correction for the number of comparisons. Disk, memory and time were measured on an Apple M3 Pro with no GPU.

The full tables on GitHub have every row with its interval, the rank-correlation versions of the tests, the construction of the German set and the commands to reproduce the page.
